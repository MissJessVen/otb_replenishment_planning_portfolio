OTB & Replenishment Planning Portfolio¶

Work sample for: Planning & Forecast Analyst (jewelry & accessories, DTC + Amazon)

This notebook runs one full monthly replenishment cycle end to end on ~1 million order lines, using the otb_planner package in src/:

  1. Data & channel drivers: clean the base, split DTC-like and marketplace-like demand
  2. Launch-and-fade curves: how fast new SKUs ramp, when they peak, when to stop rebuying
  3. Model logic & back-test: seven forecast versions, each one logic change, each one tested (one rejected)
  4. The buy: safety stock, time-phased receipts and this cycle's POs at SKU × channel level
  5. OTB reconciliation: bottom-up SKU buy vs. top-down dollar plan, with a bridge
  6. At-risk SKUs: projected stockouts, overstock, exit / markdown candidates
  7. What changed since last month: and why
  8. "Why is the model saying this?": one SKU traced from history to PO
  9. Post-mortem: replay the buy against what actually sold

Data. UCI Online Retail II (UK online giftware seller, Dec-2009 to Dec-2011, ~4,900 SKUs). It stands in for a high-SKU, short-lifecycle accessories assortment. It has no channel, cost or inventory fields, so: Domestic = UK orders (DTC proxy, 1-month lead time); International = all other countries (marketplace / FBA proxy, 2-month lead time); unit cost = 45% of selling price; opening stock is simulated (fixed seed). All assumptions live in src/otb_planner/config.py.

Cycle. Run on 31-Aug-2011 (actuals through August), planning receipts for the Sep–Nov gifting peak. The data continues to Nov-2011, which lets the post-mortem score the buy against reality.

1 · Data & channel demand drivers¶

Every exclusion is logged, so the same planning base can be rebuilt each month. Two cleaning choices matter for the forecast. First, sales fully reversed by a cancellation are netted out (this removes the famous 80,995- and 74,215-unit test orders). Second, bulk one-off B2B lines are kept in actuals but capped in the forecast base, so one wholesale order doesn't inflate next month's buy.

Read. The two channels need separate planning:

  • Seasonality differs. Christmas items lift about 10× in Domestic but about 5× International. One seasonality curve for both channels would overbuy International.
  • Basket and customer differ. International orders are bigger and almost all come from registered accounts, so a missed delivery there costs more. About a quarter of Domestic lines are guest checkout, the most DTC-like demand.

So the planning unit is SKU × channel, the same way DTC and FBA inventory pools are held separately.

2 · Launch-and-fade curves¶

I followed every SKU launched Mar-2010 to May-2011 with ≥300 units for its first 12 months. Each SKU's curve is deseasonalised with an index built from core SKUs (so assortment growth doesn't look like seasonality), smoothed, and indexed to its own peak. Each curve is then classified by shape with two transparent rules: months-to-peak and the month 5–6 level relative to peak.

Read.

  • Peaks come early. Most new SKUs peak within three months.
  • Spike-and-fade SKUs sell most of their life up front. They sell about 60% of their lifetime units in the first three months and are below 30% of peak by month 4.
  • Christmas is the extreme case. More than half of new Christmas SKUs spike and fade. Jewelry & Accessories is split evenly between spike-and-fade and gradual fade.

A single rebuy rule would either chase dead SKUs or starve the slow builders, which is why each archetype gets its own stop-rebuy rule. The forecast uses the pooled launch curve, re-estimated at each origin from history available at that date (no look-ahead).

3 · Model logic & back-test¶

Each version adds one logic change, and each change is back-tested at two origins: May (a quiet summer) and August (heading into the peak). A change ships only if it improves what the buy depends on:

  • aggregate error at category × channel × month, which is what the OTB dollars see
  • bias, which drives over- or under-buying

SKU-level WMAPE is tracked as a guardrail. For intermittent, high-SKU demand, 65–75% SKU-level error is normal.

WMAPE = Σ|F − A| / ΣA · Bias = Σ(F − A) / ΣA

Read. v1.0 under-forecast the peak by 31%.

  • Seasonality (v1.2) fixed the bias almost entirely.
  • Lifecycle, blending and top-down reconciliation (v1.3–v1.5) cut aggregate peak error from 33% to 21%.
  • v1.6a was rejected and rolled back. Applying the seasonal-returner rule to every category lowered aggregate error but pushed bias to +8% and SKU error up 5 pts, because it brought dead SKUs back into the forecast.
  • v1.6 is live. Restricting the same rule to Christmas & Seasonal cut peak aggregate error to 18% and left the May origin unchanged.

The seasonal-naive benchmark shows the underlying problem: last year's sales for a single SKU are a poor guide, even though category seasonality repeats.

4 · The buy: safety stock, receipts and this cycle's POs¶

For every SKU × channel:

  • Safety stock SS = z × σ × √(LT + R), where σ = 1.25 × MAD(ABC class) × avg forecast. z = 1.65 / 1.28 / 0.84 for A / B / C, capped at 1.28 once a SKU is fading, and 0 for exit SKUs.
  • Receipts, time-phased by month: receipt = roundup_to_case(max(0, F + target EOM − projected on hand)), with target EOM = SS + 0.5 × next month's forecast.
  • This cycle's PO lands one lead time out: Domestic (1 month) → October receipt; International (2 months, including inbound) → November receipt. September receipts are already on order.

5 · OTB reconciliation¶

OTB = planned sales + planned EOM stock − BOM stock at cost, by category × channel × month:

  • Planned sales = last year's same month × this year's Jun–Aug YoY trend.
  • Planned EOM = next month's planned sales × months of supply: 1.0 for Domestic, 1.5 for International.

Bottom-up is the sum of every SKU's receipts. Because the OTB adds up across months to Σ sales + EOM_last − BOM_first, the gap splits cleanly into a bridge.

6 · At-risk SKUs¶

Flags are simple rules, so anyone can see why a SKU is on the list:

  • Stockout risk: on hand < 50% of month-1 forecast.
  • Overstock: more than 26 weeks of supply.
  • EOL / markdown: exit or dormant SKUs still holding stock.
  • Slow mover: stock on hand with no sales in 3 months.

Recommendation & next steps¶

This cycle. Approve the Domestic October and International November POs (see outputs/buy_recommendation_2011-08.xlsx → PO_this_cycle). Chase the stockout list, starting with the Christmas seasonal returners, and move the exit list to markdown.

Decision for merchandising & finance: v1.7. Capping safety stock brings the SKU buy to within about 3% of OTB and cuts November closing stock by about $217k at cost. The trade-off is about 2 pts of unit fill rate. It's a service vs. cash call, so it ships only with sign-off. It's a one-line driver change (config.V17), and rolling back means reverting that one line.

Next logic changes to test, each with a back-test and a roll-back trigger:

  1. Archetype at launch. Classify new SKUs from their first 4–6 weeks and use that archetype's curve instead of the pooled one. New-SKU bias at the peak origin is still about −20%.
  2. New-launch OTB reserve. Hold back unallocated OTB equal to the share of peak revenue that comes from SKUs not yet launched.
  3. Stockout-censored demand. Flag months where a SKU sold out and impute true demand before it feeds the base rate.
  4. Real channel data. Replace the UK / rest-of-world proxy with Shopify vs. Seller/Vendor Central. FBA inbound and in-stock constraints then feed lead time.

Weekly model review agenda:

  • Sold vs. forecast by channel and category.
  • The top 20 misses by $, each tagged: new item, promo, stockout-censored, or real shift.
  • Launches tracking above or below their curve.
